BOTANY CHECK: “Nut” means different things in everyday cooking and in botany. Peanuts are legumes; almonds and cashews are seeds of fruits; Brazil nuts are seeds from a large capsule. This guide groups foods by how people eat them, while keeping the plant facts straight.

Beyond
the crunch.

Food facts are more useful when we know what they mean—and what they don't prove.

01

Peanuts grow underground in pods and belong to the legume family, alongside beans and lentils.

02

Almonds are the edible kernels inside the pits of fruits from the same broad plant group as peaches.

03

Brazil nuts can contain exceptionally high selenium levels. Selenium content varies a lot; more is not always better.

04

Nut nutrition depends on the variety and whether it is raw, roasted, salted, sweetened or coated.

01 / What nutrients do

Fat isn't a bad word.

Nuts commonly provide unsaturated fats, some protein, fibre and minerals. They are energy-dense, so a portion can add up quickly; that doesn't make them “bad”—it makes serving size useful context.

Whole foods also contain a mix of nutrients that cannot be captured by a single “superfood” label.

02 / Keep it real

One food can't do it all.

Nutrition numbers are averages. Cultivar, soil, storage and processing affect composition. A food's health effect also depends on the whole eating pattern, medical context and what it replaces.

Choose unsalted options when useful, store nuts cool and dry, and enjoy variety rather than chasing a single perfect nut.

03 / Allergy awareness

Serious business.

Peanut and tree-nut allergies can cause severe reactions. Peanuts and individual tree nuts are different foods, but cross-contact can occur during processing. Anyone with a diagnosed allergy should follow their clinician's avoidance plan and read labels carefully.

04 / Pantry science

Keep the crunch.

Their fats can turn rancid over time, especially with heat, light and oxygen. Keep nuts in airtight containers away from warmth; refrigeration or freezing can help extend freshness. Discard any with an off smell, taste or visible mould.

Quick-fire
questions.

Short answers to common nut and nutrition questions.

Are peanuts actually nuts?

Botanically, no. Peanuts are legumes: their pods develop underground. In kitchens and nutrition guidance they are often discussed alongside tree nuts because they are eaten in similar ways and can both be allergens.

Which nut has the most protein?

It depends on the foods being compared and the data source. Among the foods listed here, peanuts and almonds are relatively protein-rich per 100 g, but protein density alone doesn't determine the best choice for an individual.

Are raw nuts always healthier than roasted nuts?

Not automatically. Dry-roasted and raw nuts can both fit into a balanced diet. Added salt, sugar and oils change the overall product; roasting also changes flavour and can alter some heat-sensitive compounds.

Can I use these numbers for medical nutrition planning?

Use them for rough learning, not precise clinical planning. Packaged products vary, and people managing allergies, kidney conditions or other medical needs may need tailored guidance from a qualified professional.
